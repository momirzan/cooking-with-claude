# All-Purpose Pie Dough

**A superlative pie crust with butter, white vegetable fat, and egg yolk for structure**

- **Prep Time**: 15 minutes
- **Chill Time**: 1 hour minimum
- **Total Time**: 1 hour 15 minutes
- **Yield**: 2 disks (for 2 single-crust pies or 1 double-crust pie)
- **Difficulty**: Medium
- **Type**: Base/Component
- **Source**: Sam Sifton, NYT Cooking
- **Original Recipe**: https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/12319-all-purpose-pie-dough

## Description

For a superlative pie crust, start with a dough made from flour, cold butter and white vegetable fat, and scramble an egg yolk into the ice water that will bring it all together. Most pastry recipes advise strongly against "overworking" the dough, lest the crust turn tough. But it's also essential to work the dough a bit so that it will hold its shape. The yolk helps a great deal in this regard, even in its tiny measure. So does a good, strong measure of fat.

## Ingredients

- [ ] **Plain flour** (315g)
- [ ] **Unsalted butter** (90g, cold, cut into 1cm cubes)
- [ ] **White vegetable fat, e.g. Trex** (25g, cold [vegetable shortening, 2 Tbsp]) <!-- CHECK -->
- [ ] **Kosher salt** (1/4 tsp)
- [ ] **Egg yolk** (1, beaten)
- [ ] **Cider vinegar** (1 tsp)
- [ ] **Ice water** (60ml, from 180ml ice water)

## Equipment

- [ ] Food processor OR large bowl for hand mixing
- [ ] Rolling pin
- [ ] Cling film
- [ ] Kitchen scales, measuring jug and spoons

## Instructions

### Step 1: Combine Dry Ingredients and Fats
Using your fingertips or the pulse function of a food processor, blend together the flour, fats and salt until the mixture resembles a coarse meal. There should be pebbles of butter throughout the mixture.

### Step 2: Add Wet Ingredients
Add egg yolk and vinegar to 60ml ice water and stir to combine. Drizzle 60ml of this mixture over the dough and gently stir or pulse to combine.

Gather a golf ball-size bit of dough and squeeze to combine. If it does not hold together, add a little more of the liquid and stir or pulse, then check again. Repeat as necessary.

### Step 3: Work the Dough
Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured surface and gather together into a rough ball. You want to be careful not to overwork the flour, but not too careful; the dough should hold together.

Divide the ball in half with a knife or a pastry scraper, then divide each portion in half again, and again, to create eight portions. Using the heel of your hand, flatten each portion of dough once or twice to expand the pebbles of butter, then gather the dough together again in one ball. Divide this ball in half.

### Step 4: Chill
Flatten each ball into a 13cm or 15cm disc and dust lightly with flour. Wrap the discs in cling film and place in the refrigerator for at least 60 minutes.

## Notes & Tips

- **Cold is key**: Keep butter and white vegetable fat very cold for flaky layers
- **Don't fear working it**: The egg yolk helps the dough hold together even with handling
- **Pebbles of butter**: You want visible butter pieces - these create flaky layers when baked
- **Make ahead**: Dough can be refrigerated for up to 3 days or frozen for up to 3 months

## Storage

- **Refrigerator**: Up to 3 days, wrapped in cling film
- **Freezer**: Up to 3 months - thaw overnight in fridge before using
